Indonesian is one of the easiest Asian languages for travellers — no tones, mostly phonetic. A few words go a long way: locals light up when you try.

Pronunciation cheat-sheet

Read it the way you see it

A handful of rules covers almost everything. Stress sits on the second-to-last syllable.

clike ch in chair
jlike j in jam
galways hard, like go
nglike ng in singer
nylike ny in canyon

eoften a soft uh, sometimes eh

A few cultural notes

  • 01Smile and slow down. Loud, fast or impatient tone reads as rude. A warm smile and a soft permisi opens almost any door.
  • 02Right hand only. Eat, give and receive with the right hand — the left is considered unclean.
  • 03Cover up at temples. Sarong and sash are required at Balinese temples; usually provided at the entrance.
  • 04"Tidak apa-apa" (no problem) is the universal grease. Use it generously when something goes mildly sideways.
  • 05Bargain politely. At markets, start around 40–50% of the asking price and meet in the middle. Always with a smile.
  • 06Bali speaks Balinese too. Locals appreciate a Balinese "Om Swastiastu" (greetings, peace) but Bahasa Indonesia is universal.
